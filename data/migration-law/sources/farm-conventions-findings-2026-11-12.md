# Farm and livestock workers: Saudi Arabia and UAE (iMac check of official texts, 12 Nov 2026)

## (a) Saudi Arabia: Regulation for Agricultural Workers, Private Shepherds and Those Like Them

- **Decision:** Ministerial Decision 115919 of 19/08/1446H (18 Feb 2025). It takes effect on publication (art. 29). The legal basis is Labour Law art. 7(1)(d) and 7(2).
- **Sources:**
  - HRSD page: https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/knowledge-centre/decisions-and-regulations/regulation-and-procedures/لائحة-عمال-الزراعة-والرعاة-الخاصون-ومن-في-حكمهم
  - PDF: https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-03/lay-ht--mal-alzra-t-walr-at-alkhaswn-wmn-fy-hkmhm2025.pdf
  - The decision PDF is a scanned image. The date comes from its stamp and the HRSD page.

### Scope (arts 1-2)

- The regulation covers agricultural workers, nursery workers and livestock raisers.
- They must work for individuals: in homes, on private farms or pastures, or caring for private livestock. Work for establishments is not covered.
- Farm and livestock workers employed by companies therefore fall under the Labour Law.

### Rules

- **Minimum age:** 21 (art. 5).
- **Work outside the contract:** none, except through the ministry's Ajeer programme (art. 6).
- **Contract (art. 7):**
  - follows the ministry's mandatory model and is registered on the ministry platform
  - three copies: worker, employer, recruitment office
  - Arabic prevails, with a translation where one exists
  - valid even if unwritten
  - one year by default
- **Contract terms:** the contract must state hours, rest, leave and the wage (art. 8).
- **Probation:** up to 90 days, paid (art. 9).
- **Hours (art. 10):**
  - at most 8 hours of actual work a day
  - a break of at least 30 minutes after 5 hours
  - overtime paid at the hourly wage plus 50 per cent
  - all hours on holidays and Eids count as overtime
- **Weekly rest:** one paid 24-hour rest day a week (art. 11).
- **Leave (art. 12):**
  - 30 days' paid leave per 2 years
  - public holidays: Eid al-Fitr 4 days, Eid al-Adha 4 days, National Day 1 day, Founding Day 1 day
  - a return ticket every 2 years
- **Sick leave:** 15 days at full pay and 15 at half pay (art. 13).
- **Employer duties (art. 15):**
  - housing, and food or an allowance
  - transport if the worker lives away
  - no forced labour or discrimination
  - no keeping of passports or documents
  - contact with family and embassy must be allowed
  - no dangerous work
  - the employer pays recruitment, residence and related fees
- **Wages:** paid monthly in SAR into the worker's account through approved channels (arts 17-18). Deductions are capped at 25 per cent (art. 19).
- **End of contract:**
  - the contract ends at age 60, among other grounds (art. 20)
  - grounds for dismissal without benefits are listed (art. 21)
- **Leaving without notice:** the worker may leave and keep all rights after breach, ill-treatment, a change of work, or violence (art. 22).
- **End-of-service pay:** one month's wage after 4 consecutive years, plus a quarter month for each further year (art. 23).
- **Settlement of dues:** within 1 week, or 2 weeks if the worker ended the contract (art. 24).
- **Return trip:** the worker pays it only if leaving without a valid reason (art. 25).
- **Unjustified termination:** 2 months' wage as compensation (art. 26).
- **Complaints:** the ministry settles them amicably (art. 27).
- **Penalties:** those of Labour Law art. 229 apply (art. 28).
- **Not covered:**
  - changing employer or leaving the country (apart from Ajeer)
  - freedom of association
- **Agency fees:** banned separately for all recruitment by MD 119698 (2025) art. 42.

## (b) UAE: Federal Decree-Law 33/2021 (Labour Law) and Cabinet Resolution 1/2022

- **Sources:**
  - FDL 33/2021: https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541 (in force 2 Feb 2022, three amendments)
  - CR 1/2022: https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1547
- **Scope (art. 3):**
  - The law applies to all private-sector establishments, employers and workers.
  - It excludes government employees, the armed forces, the police and security services, and domestic workers.
  - The Cabinet may exclude other categories, but no farm exclusion was found.
- **No farm exclusion:** a full-text search for agricult*, farm, grazing, shepherd, herd and livestock found nothing.
  - The 1980 law excluded farming and grazing workers. That exclusion has been dropped.
- **Who is covered:**
  - Farm workers employed by companies are covered by the Labour Law.
  - Household farm and livestock workers fall under FDL 9/2022 (farmer, gardener, household shepherd, horse groomer and falcon tamer are listed occupations).
