## 3. How to work (rules for every task)

1. **Trusted sources only:** government legislation databases, court websites and (if approved) official agency pages. No news, blogs or NGO reports as proof of law; they can be cited only as "reports say".
2. **Quote, don't paraphrase:** for each cell, copy the provision (under 40 words) in the original language, add an English gloss, the URL and the version or amendment date shown on the page.
3. **Save the page:** store a PDF or HTML copy in raw/ with the date in the file name.
4. **Never change a value alone:** propose a new value in the worksheet with your reason; a second reader confirms it, and the director approves any recode.
5. **Fill the worksheet columns:** official_url_found, article_quoted, matches (yes / no / unclear), correct_value_if_not, comment, editor, second reader, date.
6. **Return the worksheet** to the OneDrive folder; Claude merges it into the site data, updates the version and change log, and rebuilds the site.

## 4. Larger pieces of work for later

- **Independent second coding.** The method page promises a full second coding of all 70 cells with agreement reported as Cohen's kappa. Two editors code blind from the codebook alone, then compare. This is the main step to make the data publishable in a journal.
- **Quarterly upkeep.** Every three months, check the 26 laws for new amendments (dates and links) and the annual wage figures, and log changes.
- **New groups.** Candidates: Japan's new Employment for Skill Development system (replacing technical intern training from 2027), Taiwan's caregivers, Korea's seasonal farm workers (E-8).
- **Indonesian review.** A native speaker checks the Indonesian notes and method text after each update.
