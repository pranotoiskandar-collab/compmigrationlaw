Injury, illness or death at work: court decisions and law on migrant workers

Claims for disability and death benefits, medical costs and compensation, including seafarers’ claims under standard employment contracts and claims against insurers or welfare funds in the country of origin. The IMR databases hold 100 court decisions (14 full summaries, 86 unchecked candidates) on compensation for migrant workers injured, ill or killed at work, from national, regional and international courts.

Relevant standards: ILO Equality of Treatment (Accident Compensation) Convention, 1925 (No. 19); ILO Maritime Labour Convention, 2006

Decisions with full summaries

  • Writ on Bangladeshi workers who died on World Cup projects High Court Division, Supreme Court of Bangladesh · Bangladesh · 2023 · advisory

    A lawyer asked Bangladesh’s High Court to make the government account for workers who died or were hurt building stadiums abroad. The court ordered a list and asked about compensation. A higher judge paused part of the order.

  • Public Prosecutor v. Gaiyathiri d/o Murugayan High Court of Singapore · Singapore · 2021 · worker side won

    A young domestic worker from Myanmar was starved and beaten to death by her employer in Singapore. The High Court sentenced the employer to life in prison. Others in the household were also prosecuted.

  • Mahlangu v. Minister of Labour Constitutional Court of South Africa · South Africa · 2020 · worker side won

    A domestic worker drowned in her employer’s pool and her family got nothing because the law left out domestic workers. The Constitutional Court said that was discrimination. The ruling covers many migrant domestic workers too.

  • Public Prosecutor v. Ambika M.A. Shan (death of Adelina Lisao) High Court, Penang (Malaysia) · Malaysia · 2019 · worker side lost

    Adelina, a young Indonesian domestic worker, died in Malaysia after being made to sleep outside with a dog and left with untreated wounds. The employer was freed when the prosecution dropped the case, causing protests in both countries.

  • Public Prosecutor v. Tay Wee Kiat and Chua Ying Ling High Court of Singapore · Singapore · 2018 · worker side won

    A couple in Singapore abused their domestic worker from Myanmar for months. On appeal the High Court gave them longer prison terms.

  • Chowdury and Others v. Greece European Court of Human Rights · Greece · 2017 · worker side won

    Workers from Bangladesh picked strawberries in Greece for months without pay. When they asked for their wages, a supervisor shot at them. A Greek court cleared the employers of trafficking. The European Court said Greece failed to protect the workers from forced labour and ordered compensation.

  • Sentencia SU-677/17 (emergency health care for migrants without status) Constitutional Court of Colombia · Colombia · 2017 · worker side won

    A pregnant woman from Venezuela without legal status in Colombia was refused prenatal care. The Constitutional Court said everyone, with or without papers, has a right to urgent health care, understood broadly.

  • Undocumented Workers v. United States (Report No. 50/16) Inter-American Commission on Human Rights · United States · 2016 · worker side won

    Undocumented workers injured at work or not paid in the US took their case to the Inter-American Commission. It said the US must give them the same labour protection as others.

  • HKSAR v. Law Wan-tung (Erwiana Sulistyaningsih case) District Court, Hong Kong · Hong Kong · 2015 · worker side won

    An Indonesian domestic worker was badly beaten by her employer and sent home barely able to walk. Her case caused protests. The court convicted the employer and sent her to prison for six years.

  • HKSAR v. Tai Chi-wai and Catherine Au Yuk-shan (Kartika Puspitasari case) District Court, Hong Kong · Hong Kong · 2013 · worker side won

    An Indonesian domestic worker was abused for two years by her employers. A Hong Kong court convicted both employers and sent them to prison.

  • Becmen Service Exporter v. Cuaresma Supreme Court of the Philippines · Philippines · 2009 · worker side won

    A young Filipina nurse died in Saudi Arabia and the employer called it suicide. Her parents sued. The Supreme Court held the recruitment agency and the employer liable to the family.

  • Public Prosecutor v. Yim Pek Ha (Nirmala Bonat case) Sessions Court, Kuala Lumpur (Malaysia) · Malaysia · 2008 · worker side won

    Nirmala, a domestic worker from Indonesia, was burned with an iron and scalded by her employer in Kuala Lumpur. The court convicted the employer and sent her to prison.

  • Lost earnings of an injured worker without a visa Supreme Court of Japan · Japan · 1997 · worker side won

    A Pakistani worker without a visa was injured in a Japanese factory. The Supreme Court said the employer must pay damages, counting Japanese wages for a limited time and home-country wages after that.

  • Undocumented worker and industrial accident compensation Supreme Court of Korea · South Korea · 1995 · worker side won

    A foreign worker without the right papers was hurt at a factory in Korea and refused compensation. The Supreme Court said immigration status does not cancel his right to accident benefits.
