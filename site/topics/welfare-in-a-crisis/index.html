Food and support in a crisis: court decisions and law on migrant workers

Decisions on relief for migrants during emergencies such as the COVID-19 lockdowns, including internal migrant workers. The IMR databases hold 21 court decisions (15 full summaries, 6 unchecked candidates) on food, shelter and social support for migrants in a crisis, from national, regional and international courts.

Relevant standards: ICESCR Arts. 9 and 11

  • 3regional courts and bodies
  • 15countries

Decisions with full summaries

  • Writ on Bangladeshi workers who died on World Cup projects High Court Division, Supreme Court of Bangladesh · Bangladesh · 2023 · advisory

    A lawyer asked Bangladesh’s High Court to make the government account for workers who died or were hurt building stadiums abroad. The court ordered a list and asked about compensation. A higher judge paused part of the order.

  • In Re: Problems and Miseries of Migrant Labourers Supreme Court of India · India · 2021 · worker side won

    During COVID-19, millions of workers who had moved within India lost their jobs and could not get food rations away from home. The Supreme Court ordered the government to register them and let them use their ration card anywhere in the country.

  • Order to use the Foreign Employment Welfare Fund for repatriation Supreme Court of Nepal · Nepal · 2020 · worker side won

    During COVID-19, many Nepali workers lost their jobs abroad and could not pay for a flight home. The Supreme Court ordered the government to use the welfare fund that workers pay into to bring them back.

  • National Campaign Committee for Central Legislation on Construction Labour v. Union of India Supreme Court of India · India · 2018 · worker side won

    India collects a levy on building projects for construction workers, many of them migrants. The Supreme Court found most of the money was not reaching them and ordered the government to register workers and use the funds properly.

  • Social assistance for foreign residents (RE 587.970) Supreme Federal Court of Brazil · Brazil · 2017 · worker side won

    An Italian woman who had lived in Brazil for decades was refused the old-age benefit because she was not Brazilian. The Supreme Court said residents are entitled to it like citizens.

  • Permanent residents and public assistance Supreme Court of Japan · Japan · 2014 · worker side lost

    An elderly Chinese woman who had lived in Japan for decades was refused welfare. The Supreme Court said foreigners have no legal right to welfare, though the government may give it voluntarily.

  • Kong Yunming v. Director of Social Welfare Court of Final Appeal, Hong Kong · Hong Kong · 2013 · worker side won

    A woman who moved from mainland China to Hong Kong was refused welfare because she had not lived there seven years. The highest court said this rule was unconstitutional.

  • Andrejeva v. Latvia (Grand Chamber) European Court of Human Rights · Latvia · 2009 · worker side won

    A long-term resident of Latvia without citizenship got a smaller pension than citizens for the same work history. The Grand Chamber said this was discrimination.

  • Reyes Aguilera, Daniela c/ Estado Nacional Supreme Court of Argentina · Argentina · 2007 · worker side won

    A girl from Bolivia with a severe disability had lived in Argentina since 1999. She was refused a disability pension because foreigners needed 20 years of residence. The Supreme Court said that rule was unconstitutional.

  • Khosa v. Minister of Social Development Constitutional Court of South Africa · South Africa · 2004 · worker side won

    Mozambicans who were permanent residents in South Africa were denied pensions and child grants because they were not citizens. The Constitutional Court said they must be included.

  • J.Y. Interpretation No. 560 (funeral allowance for foreign workers’ family members) Constitutional Court of Taiwan (Judicial Yuan) · Taiwan · 2003 · worker side lost

    A foreign worker in Taiwan asked for the funeral allowance that Taiwanese workers get when a family member dies. The rules excluded family who lived abroad. The Constitutional Court let the rule stand.

  • Koua Poirrez v. France European Court of Human Rights · France · 2003 · worker side won

    A man from Côte d’Ivoire with a disability, living lawfully in France, was refused an allowance because he was not French or from a treaty country. The Court found discrimination.

  • Gaygusuz v. Austria European Court of Human Rights · Austria · 1996 · worker side won

    A Turkish worker in Austria who had paid into the system was refused emergency benefits because he was not Austrian. The European Court said this was discrimination.

  • Neeraja Chaudhary v. State of Madhya Pradesh Supreme Court of India · India · 1984 · worker side won

    Workers brought from other states to quarries near Delhi were freed from debt bondage but left with nothing. The Supreme Court said the state must also help them start again.

  • Graham v. Richardson Supreme Court of the United States · United States · 1971 · worker side won

    Arizona and Pennsylvania denied welfare to non-citizen residents. The Supreme Court said treating lawful residents worse because they are not citizens needs strong justification, which was missing.
