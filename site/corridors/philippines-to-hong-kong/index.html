Filipino migrant workers in Hong Kong: law, court decisions and legal help

What the law says for workers who migrate from Philippines to Hong Kong: agreements between the two states, the labor and migration law of each side, court decisions from both, the treaties each has joined, and where to find free legal help. Size of the corridor: 205,764 foreign domestic helpers in end 2025 (official statistics of the destination, see East Asia corridors data).

Court decisions on this corridor

Decisions from courts in Hong Kong that name Filipino workers, and decisions from courts in Philippines that concern work in Hong Kong (found by text search of the summaries).

  • Comilang v. Director of Immigration Court of Final Appeal, Hong Kong · Hong Kong · 2019 · worker side lost

    Former domestic workers from the Philippines and Indonesia had children who are Hong Kong permanent residents. They asked to stay to raise them. The highest court said they had no right to stay.

  • Vallejos v. Commissioner of Registration Court of Final Appeal, Hong Kong · Hong Kong · 2013 · worker side lost

    A domestic worker from the Philippines had worked in Hong Kong for many years. Most people who live there for seven years can become permanent residents, but the law says time as a foreign domestic helper does not count. The highest court said that rule is allowed.

  • EMS Manpower and Placement Services v. NLRC Supreme Court of the Philippines · Philippines · 1997 · Unchecked candidate

Court decisions by topic, in both countries

Unpaid wages: Hong Kong (1)

  • HKSAR v. Law Wan-tung (Erwiana Sulistyaningsih case) District Court, Hong Kong · 2015 · worker side won

    An Indonesian domestic worker was badly beaten by her employer and sent home barely able to walk. Her case caused protests. The court convicted the employer and sent her to prison for six years.

Unpaid wages: Philippines (45)

  • Sameer Overseas Placement Agency v. Cabiles Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2014 · worker side won

    A woman recruited in the Philippines for factory work in Taiwan was sent home early. The Supreme Court said the limit on her claim was still unfair, even though Congress had written it into the law again, and that the agency that recruited her must pay together with the employer.

  • PERT/CPM Manpower Exponent Co. v. Vinuya Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2012 · worker side won

    Filipino workers sent abroad were made to sign new contracts with lower pay than promised, then quit and came home. The Supreme Court said they had effectively been dismissed and the agency had to pay.

  • Yap v. Thenamaris Ship’s Management Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2011 · worker side won

    A Filipino seafarer was let go early when his ship was sold. The Supreme Court applied its earlier Serrano ruling and said he must be paid for the rest of his contract.

Recruitment fees and agencies: Philippines (201)

  • Sto. Tomas v. Salac Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2012 · worker side won

    Recruitment agencies argued it was unfair to make their directors personally pay workers’ claims. The Supreme Court said the rule is valid because it protects workers abroad.

  • People v. Lalli Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2011 · worker side won

    Women from the Philippines were promised jobs in Malaysia but were forced into prostitution. The Supreme Court upheld the recruiters’ convictions.

  • Becmen Service Exporter v. Cuaresma Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2009 · worker side won

    A young Filipina nurse died in Saudi Arabia and the employer called it suicide. Her parents sued. The Supreme Court held the recruitment agency and the employer liable to the family.

Dismissed or sent home: Philippines (40)

  • Saudi Arabian Airlines v. Rebesencio Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2015 · worker side won

    A foreign airline forced Filipino cabin crew to resign when they became pregnant. The Supreme Court said they could sue at home and that their dismissal was unlawful.

  • Serrano v. Gallant Maritime Services Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2009 · worker side won

    A Filipino seafarer was sent home before his contract ended. The law limited what he could claim to three months’ pay. The Supreme Court said this limit treated overseas workers unfairly and struck it down, so he could claim pay for the rest of his contract.

  • Melba Alcantara Denusta v. Migrant Workers Manpower Agency Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2024 · Unchecked candidate

Forced labor and trafficking: Hong Kong (4)

  • ZN v. Secretary for Justice Court of Final Appeal, Hong Kong · 2020 · worker side lost

    A Pakistani man said he was made to work for years in Hong Kong without pay. He argued Hong Kong must have a crime of human trafficking. The highest court disagreed.

  • HKSAR v. Tai Chi-wai and Catherine Au Yuk-shan (Kartika Puspitasari case) District Court, Hong Kong · 2013 · worker side won

    An Indonesian domestic worker was abused for two years by her employers. A Hong Kong court convicted both employers and sent them to prison.

Arrested, detained or expelled: Hong Kong (8)

  • Ghulam Rbani v. Secretary for Justice Court of Final Appeal, Hong Kong · 2014 · worker side won

    A Pakistani man was held for weeks by Hong Kong immigration officers without anyone deciding whether he needed to be held. The top court said that part of his detention was unlawful.

  • C v. Director of Immigration Court of Final Appeal, Hong Kong · 2013 · worker side won

    Hong Kong had left refugee claims to the UN refugee agency. The top court said the government must judge the risk itself before sending anyone away.

  • Ubamaka v. Secretary for Security Court of Final Appeal, Hong Kong · 2012 · worker side lost

    A Nigerian man who had served a prison sentence in Hong Kong feared a second prosecution at home. The top court said the protection against cruel treatment applies to deportation, but not on these facts.

Residence and staying on: Hong Kong (20)

  • Director of Immigration v. Chong Fung Yuen Court of Final Appeal, Hong Kong · 2001 · worker side won

    A boy was born in Hong Kong to parents from mainland China who were visiting. The top court said he had the right to live in Hong Kong because he was born there.

  • Ng Ka Ling v. Director of Immigration Court of Final Appeal, Hong Kong · 1999 · worker side won

    Thousands of children in mainland China had a parent living in Hong Kong. The top court said they had the right to live in Hong Kong. Months later, an interpretation by China’s legislature limited the effect of the ruling.

  • Hksar v. Li Qingbin (李慶彬) Court of Final Appeal, Hong Kong · 2025 · Unchecked candidate

Residence and staying on: Philippines (2)

  • Macalintal v. Commission on Elections (overseas absentee voting) Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2003 · worker side won

    A lawyer challenged the new law letting Filipinos abroad vote. The Supreme Court upheld the right of overseas workers to vote from abroad.
