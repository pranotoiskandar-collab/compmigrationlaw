Court decisions on this corridor

Decisions from courts in Malaysia that name Nepali workers, and decisions from courts in Nepal that concern work in Malaysia (found by text search of the summaries).

  • Directive to enforce the “free visa, free ticket” policy Supreme Court of Nepal · Nepal · 2019 · worker side won

    Nepal decided in 2015 that employers abroad should pay for workers’ visas and tickets, but agencies kept charging workers. The Supreme Court told the government to make the rule work.

Court decisions by topic, in both countries

Unpaid wages: Malaysia (2)

  • 93 migrant workers left without jobs on arrival High Court, Shah Alam (Malaysia) · 2024 · worker side won

    Ninety-three workers arrived in Malaysia in 2023 and found there were no jobs for them. The Labour Court ordered the employer to pay them, and the High Court agreed.

  • Fice Fransina Nenobais v. Lee Hee Chooi Court of Appeal, Malaysia · 2022 · worker side won

    An Indonesian domestic worker in Malaysia went unpaid for almost five years. The labour office refused her claim because she had no valid permit. The courts said she could still claim her wages.

Dismissed or sent home: Nepal (1)

  • Order to use the Foreign Employment Welfare Fund for repatriation Supreme Court of Nepal · 2020 · worker side won

    During COVID-19, many Nepali workers lost their jobs abroad and could not pay for a flight home. The Supreme Court ordered the government to use the welfare fund that workers pay into to bring them back.

Forced labor and trafficking: Malaysia (2)

  • Public Prosecutor v. Ambika M.A. Shan (death of Adelina Lisao) High Court, Penang (Malaysia) · 2019 · worker side lost

    Adelina, a young Indonesian domestic worker, died in Malaysia after being made to sleep outside with a dog and left with untreated wounds. The employer was freed when the prosecution dropped the case, causing protests in both countries.

  • Public Prosecutor v. Yim Pek Ha (Nirmala Bonat case) Sessions Court, Kuala Lumpur (Malaysia) · 2008 · worker side won

    Nirmala, a domestic worker from Indonesia, was burned with an iron and scalded by her employer in Kuala Lumpur. The court convicted the employer and sent her to prison.
