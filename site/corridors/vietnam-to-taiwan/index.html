Court decisions by topic, in both countries

Arrested, detained or expelled: Taiwan (2)

  • J.Y. Interpretation No. 708 (detention of foreigners pending deportation) Constitutional Court of Taiwan (Judicial Yuan) · 2013 · worker side won

    Taiwan held foreigners, including migrant workers who had left their employers, in detention before deportation without a judge checking. The Constitutional Court said a court must be able to review such detention quickly.

  • J.Y. Interpretation No. 710 (deportation and detention of mainland Chinese residents) Constitutional Court of Taiwan (Judicial Yuan) · 2013 · worker side won

    Following its decision on foreigners, the Constitutional Court said people from mainland China must also get a hearing before removal and a quick court check on any detention.

Residence and staying on: Taiwan (4)

  • J.Y. Interpretation No. 497 (entry and residence of people from mainland China) Constitutional Court of Taiwan (Judicial Yuan) · 1999 · worker side lost

    Taiwan limited how people from mainland China could come and stay. The Constitutional Court said these limits were allowed.

  • J.Y. Interpretation No. 712 Constitutional Court of Taiwan (Judicial Yuan) · 2013 · Unchecked candidate

Treated worse as a foreigner: Taiwan (3)

  • J.Y. Interpretation No. 618 (former mainland spouses and public office) Constitutional Court of Taiwan (Judicial Yuan) · 2006 · worker side lost

    A woman from mainland China married a Taiwanese man, became a resident and was hired as a civil servant, then dismissed under the ten-year rule. The Constitutional Court let the rule stand.

  • J.Y. Interpretation No. 560 (funeral allowance for foreign workers’ family members) Constitutional Court of Taiwan (Judicial Yuan) · 2003 · worker side lost

    A foreign worker in Taiwan asked for the funeral allowance that Taiwanese workers get when a family member dies. The rules excluded family who lived abroad. The Constitutional Court let the rule stand.
