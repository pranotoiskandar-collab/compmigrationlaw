MIGRATION JURISPRUDENCE FROM THE SOUTH

Migrant workers in East Asia

Taiwan, South Korea, Hong Kong and Japan rely on temporary migrant workers in jobs their own citizens leave. This comparative overview covers four groups in particular: migrant fishers in Taiwan and South Korea, with a focus on Taiwan's distant-water fleet; foreign domestic helpers in Hong Kong; and technical intern trainees and specified skilled workers in Japan. It codes how the law applies to each group, maps the treaties, importing-state measures and bilateral agreements that bear on them, and lets you explore and download the data.

I · AT A GLANCE

How the law applies to each group

The unit of comparison is the worker group, not the state, because each jurisdiction runs separate legal regimes for different migrants. The sharpest contrast lies within Taiwan: fishers hired in Taiwan fall under the Labor Standards Act, while crew recruited abroad for the distant-water fleet are governed by fisheries regulations with their own, lower wage floor.

II · COMPARE

Build your own comparison

Choose which features matter for your question by moving the sliders. A weighted comparison appears once you set a weight. Each feature counts 2 when the protection is full, 1 when partial and 0 when absent, following the codebook in section XI.

Check how stable any ranking is: 10,000 random weightings

Each weighting draws every weight uniformly between 0 and 3 (fixed seed, so the result is reproducible). A group that scores highest under most weightings does so regardless of what one values; a split result means the comparison depends on the weights.

III · INTERNATIONAL LAYER

Treaties, and what stands in for them

Taiwan is not a member of the United Nations or the International Labour Organization and cannot ratify their treaties. It has instead written the two UN covenants into domestic law, and its fishing labor rules are increasingly shaped by importing states: the United States lists Taiwan-caught fish as produced with forced labor, and the European Union's yellow card of 2015 to 2019 and its new Forced Labour Regulation reach the fleet through trade. Hong Kong is bound through extension by the United Kingdom before 1997 and by China since.

VI · FRAMEWORKS AND AGREEMENTS

The normative framework, by level and nature

Instruments that bind or guide the four jurisdictions on these workers, classified by level (international, regional fisheries bodies, measures by importing states, bilateral) and by nature. Importing-state measures are listed as a level of their own because, for Taiwan's fleet, they do much of the work that treaties do elsewhere. Click a cell in the map to filter the list.

A memorandum of understanding usually creates no binding legal obligation; a treaty or agreement does once in force. A measure of a regional fisheries management organization binds its members and cooperating parties, including Taiwan as a fishing entity.

    XI · METHOD AND CITATION

    How the data was built

    Use of AI: AI is used to re-confirm that none of the data we use infringes intellectual property law, to test our labels and to edit summaries into plain language, which people then re-edit. How we use AI

    The unit of analysis is the legal regime that applies to a defined group of migrant workers, on paper (de jure), as of the date shown. Enforcement (de facto) is not coded; where reports show a gap between law and practice, the note to the cell says so. Eight features concern labor protection; two (a route to long-term residence and family accompaniment) concern immigration status, following the categories of the International Organization for Migration's International Comparative Study of Migration Legislation and Practice (2002). Each cell is coded by the written rule below and records its note, its source, the date coded and whether it rests on the primary legal text (P) or on a secondary source or a statutory citation not yet checked against the official text (S).

    Limitations: official English translations are unofficial in law and the original text prevails; regulations and ministerial notices change faster than statutes; official statistics on fishers are sparse and dated, and the populations counted differ between jurisdictions. Treaty status should be confirmed in NORMLEX and the UN Treaty Collection before citation.

    Reliability and replication

    • Coding protocol: one coder applied the rules above to 70 cells (7 groups × 10 features). On 31 October 2026 a second reader checked 30 cells against the official legal texts in the original language, quoting the provision, and one cell was recoded. On 13 November 2026 all 70 cells were checked again on official government and court sites only: checked cells are marked P ("partly" where part of the note still rests on a secondary source or on our reading), and three cells were recoded. A full independent second coding is planned, with agreement reported as Cohen's kappa. Cells marked S should be treated as provisional.
    • Feature selection: the eight labor features follow the core ILO standards for fishing (C188) and domestic work (C189). The mobility, residence and family features follow the restrictions that define temporary labor migration programs in Asia, namely a visa tied to one employer, no change of sector, no route to long-term residence and no family reunion (Oberoi and Sheill 2024; OHCHR 2022), and the immigration-law categories of IOM (2002).
    • Corridor analyses treat each origin–destination pair as a case. Pairs within one jurisdiction are not independent and each jurisdiction counts a different population, so the presets include the destination as a control; the p-values do not correct for clustering and should be read as descriptive.
    • Many tests can be run on the same data. Treat any single significant result as a lead to investigate, not a finding; the statistics engine applies Holm's correction to pairwise comparisons.
    • Any weighted comparison depends on the weights chosen. The stability check under the comparison shows how often each group scores highest across 10,000 random weightings.
    • Replication files: the codebook, every coded cell with its note and source, treaty status, instruments, domestic laws, corridors and developments can be downloaded below as CSV and JSON. Each release carries a version number and a dated change log.

    For researchers

    This comparison is built for empirical legal scholarship and socio-legal research on migration and the rights of migrant workers, as well as for advocacy.

    The coded variables support statistical analysis in law: cross-national comparison, regression and event-history designs, and law and economics questions such as how recruitment fees, wage protection and limits on changing employer affect migrant workers. The data can be downloaded for replication.
