Dismissed or sent home: migrant worker court decisions in Philippines

Court decisions from Philippines on dismissal and early repatriation of migrant workers: 42 court decisions (5 full summaries, 37 unchecked candidates). Claims after a worker was dismissed or sent home before the end of the contract: compensation for the unexpired term, notice, and who pays for the return journey.

Relevant standards: ICRMW Art. 54; ILO Termination of Employment Convention, 1982 (No. 158)

Decisions

  • Saudi Arabian Airlines v. Rebesencio Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2015 · worker side won

    A foreign airline forced Filipino cabin crew to resign when they became pregnant. The Supreme Court said they could sue at home and that their dismissal was unlawful.

  • Sameer Overseas Placement Agency v. Cabiles Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2014 · worker side won

    A woman recruited in the Philippines for factory work in Taiwan was sent home early. The Supreme Court said the limit on her claim was still unfair, even though Congress had written it into the law again, and that the agency that recruited her must pay together with the employer.

  • PERT/CPM Manpower Exponent Co. v. Vinuya Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2012 · worker side won

    Filipino workers sent abroad were made to sign new contracts with lower pay than promised, then quit and came home. The Supreme Court said they had effectively been dismissed and the agency had to pay.

  • Yap v. Thenamaris Ship’s Management Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2011 · worker side won

    A Filipino seafarer was let go early when his ship was sold. The Supreme Court applied its earlier Serrano ruling and said he must be paid for the rest of his contract.

  • Serrano v. Gallant Maritime Services Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2009 · worker side won

    A Filipino seafarer was sent home before his contract ended. The law limited what he could claim to three months’ pay. The Supreme Court said this limit treated overseas workers unfairly and struck it down, so he could claim pay for the rest of his contract.
