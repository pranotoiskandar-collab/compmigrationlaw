Recruitment fees and agencies: migrant worker court decisions in Philippines

Court decisions from Philippines on recruitment fees, illegal recruitment and agency liability: 200 court decisions (7 full summaries, 193 unchecked candidates). Decisions on fees charged to workers, licensing of recruitment agencies, illegal recruitment, and the liability of agencies for the obligations of foreign employers.

Relevant standards: ILO Private Employment Agencies Convention, 1997 (No. 181) Art. 7; ILO General Principles and Operational Guidelines for Fair Recruitment (2019)

Decisions

  • Sameer Overseas Placement Agency v. Cabiles Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2014 · worker side won

    A woman recruited in the Philippines for factory work in Taiwan was sent home early. The Supreme Court said the limit on her claim was still unfair, even though Congress had written it into the law again, and that the agency that recruited her must pay together with the employer.

  • PERT/CPM Manpower Exponent Co. v. Vinuya Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2012 · worker side won

    Filipino workers sent abroad were made to sign new contracts with lower pay than promised, then quit and came home. The Supreme Court said they had effectively been dismissed and the agency had to pay.

  • Sto. Tomas v. Salac Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2012 · worker side won

    Recruitment agencies argued it was unfair to make their directors personally pay workers’ claims. The Supreme Court said the rule is valid because it protects workers abroad.

  • People v. Lalli Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2011 · worker side won

    Women from the Philippines were promised jobs in Malaysia but were forced into prostitution. The Supreme Court upheld the recruiters’ convictions.

  • Becmen Service Exporter v. Cuaresma Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2009 · worker side won

    A young Filipina nurse died in Saudi Arabia and the employer called it suicide. Her parents sued. The Supreme Court held the recruitment agency and the employer liable to the family.

  • Datuman v. First Cosmopolitan Manpower Supreme Court of the Philippines · 2008 · worker side won

    A Filipina sent to Saudi Arabia was made to sign a new contract with worse pay and work. The Supreme Court said the agency in the Philippines must pay what she lost.

  • JMM Promotion and Management v. Court of Appeals Supreme Court of the Philippines · 1996 · worker side lost

    After a Filipina performer died in Japan, the government added training and a record book for performers going abroad. Agencies said this was unfair. The Supreme Court upheld the rule.
